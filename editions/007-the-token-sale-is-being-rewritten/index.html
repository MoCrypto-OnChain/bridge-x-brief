The Token Sale Is Being Rewritten.

The SEC has proposed Regulation Crypto Assets: a tailored framework for covered investment contracts involving crypto assets, with a startup exemption, a larger fundraising exemption and a proposed safe harbor. The direction is clearer. The details still matter.

Bridge X Capital · August 24, 2026 · Education by Default · Primary-Source Receipts · PoW

$5M Startup Exemption$75M Fundraising ExemptionSafe HarborDisclosure & ReportingSecondary-Market Treatment

On August 18, 2026, the Securities and Exchange Commission proposed a new regulation specifically for certain crypto-asset transactions. The proposal does not declare every token sale lawful, and it does not erase the securities laws. Instead, it attempts to create purpose-built routes for capital formation where a crypto asset is offered as part of an investment contract.

The change is not “tokens are no longer regulated.” The change is that the SEC is proposing a more explicit map for how certain token-related offerings can move from capital formation toward functioning network assets.

Design & Structure: first identify what is being offered

The proposal uses the concept of a covered investment contract. That matters because the legal analysis can distinguish the crypto asset itself from the transaction or contractual arrangement through which purchasers are asked to invest.

For builders, this makes architecture inseparable from distribution. Token utility, issuer promises, development obligations, economic expectations, governance rights and the manner of sale all contribute to the regulatory picture.

Offering designWhat purchasers are promised when capital is raised.
Network maturityHow dependent the asset remains on managerial efforts.
Distribution structureWho receives the asset, when, on what terms and with what restrictions.

One-time startup exemption: up to $5 million

Proposed Subpart B would create a startup exemption from Securities Act registration for certain offers, sales and other distributions of covered investment contracts. The exemption would allow up to $5 million during a period of up to four years.

The strategic idea is runway: let a project raise a limited amount while it develops toward a more mature network, but require enough disclosure for purchasers and regulators to understand what is being financed.

Bridge X read: this is not a “free token sale” lane. It is a proposed exempt-offering lane with conditions, disclosure and a defined time-and-dollar perimeter.

Annual fundraising exemption: up to $75 million

Proposed Subpart C would create a broader fundraising exemption permitting offerings of up to $75 million in each 12-month period. The SEC describes the mechanics as modeled in significant part on Regulation A and proposes two tiers with different limits and requirements.

That larger ceiling makes the framework relevant beyond very early-stage projects. It also increases the importance of financial condition, financial statements, offering disclosures and ongoing compliance discipline.

Disclosure & investor protections do not disappear

The proposal repeatedly ties exempt pathways to disclosure. A tailored regime only works if purchasers can distinguish network claims from marketing claims and operating facts from future aspirations.

  • Project and asset information: what exists today versus what remains under development.
  • Use of proceeds: what raised capital is intended to fund.
  • Financial condition: particularly under the larger fundraising pathway.
  • Material risks: including technical, governance, market, custody and regulatory risk.
  • Ongoing reporting: where the selected exemption requires it.
Utility is not a substitute for disclosure. A useful network can still be financed through a transaction that creates securities-law obligations.

Safe harbor: when the network asset separates from the investment contract

Proposed Subpart D would establish a safe harbor from the term “investment contract” for certain crypto assets when specified conditions are satisfied. This is the proposal’s most consequential conceptual move because it addresses the possibility that an asset associated with an investment contract can later be treated differently from the capital-raising arrangement around it.

That does not mean every asset “graduates” automatically. The conditions and factual record matter. Network functionality, the continuing role of managerial efforts, disclosures, distribution facts and market conduct can all affect the analysis.

Secondary-market treatment: the hard question moves downstream

If primary issuance gets clearer pathways, secondary markets still need to know what exactly is trading. The SEC’s 2026 interpretive work has already focused on when a non-security crypto asset can be offered or sold as part of an investment contract and when the asset may be viewed separately from that contract.

That separation question is operationally important for exchanges, broker-dealers, market makers, custodians and projects because market participants need rules that can be applied in real time.

Network Utility & Compliance: design for the life after fundraising

The strongest implication for serious builders is that compliance cannot be bolted on after a token sale. The offering pathway, network architecture, governance, treasury, disclosures and post-launch communications should be designed as one system.

A project that raises capital under an exemption but then behaves as though the exemption erased every downstream obligation has misunderstood the direction of travel. The proposed framework is attempting to make compliant innovation more workable, not consequence-free.

The Bridge X operating model

For builders, the practical sequence is becoming easier to articulate:

1 · DesignDefine real network utility, control, governance and economic function.
2 · Choose pathwayMatch fundraising structure to the applicable legal route and limits.
3 · DiscloseSeparate present facts, future plans, risks and financial information.
4 · OperateMaintain records, reporting, distribution controls and post-launch discipline.

The proposal remains a proposal. It is subject to public comment and could change before adoption. Congress also remains relevant because agency rulemaking cannot substitute permanently for statutory market-structure legislation.

The token sale is being rewritten—but the winning model is not “less discipline.” It is better alignment between capital formation, disclosure, network utility and market structure.
